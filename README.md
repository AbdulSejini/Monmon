# Monmon

Learning materials for a Grade 6 student (Class 6.3).

## Dashboard

[`index.html`](index.html) is the home page, in Arabic: what he has to do (homework not yet due, grouped by due date, with ticks), his upcoming tests with what to study (and a link to the study page when there is one), links to the practice pages, and every earlier week's homework and tests.

The homework and tests come from the school's public EduPage list for class 6.3 (`https://aisedusa.edupage.org/exam/`), which needs no login. Grades, attendance and messages need a login and are not included.

- `tools/edupage_sync.py` re-reads that list and rewrites the data block in `index.html` (standard library only; `--dry-run` prints the items without changing anything). The block keeps two times: `synced`, when the list last changed, and `checked`, when it was last read (stamped at least once a day). If the list hasn't been read for two days, the dashboard shows a warning at the top.
- `.github/workflows/edupage-sync.yml` runs it every hour from 05:41 to 23:41 Jeddah time and commits only when something changed, plus one "EduPage checked: no changes" commit a day. GitHub often starts scheduled runs late or skips some, so the hourly schedule keeps the site close to EduPage. EduPage refuses connections from many of GitHub's machines (from 29 Oct, every run on GitHub's usual Linux machines failed to connect), so the sync runs on a macOS machine, and when EduPage can't be read from there it tries once more from an ARM Linux machine, which has other addresses. If neither can reach it, the next run tries again, and the workflow only fails (with GitHub's failure email) once the list hasn't been read for 24 hours. The workflow can also be started by hand from the Actions tab ("Sync EduPage", then "Run workflow").
- The site is published with GitHub Pages at <https://abdulsejini.github.io/Monmon/>, built from the root of the main branch (`.nojekyll` serves the files as they are). Any commit to the main branch, including the sync's, goes live within a couple of minutes.
- Every page, the dashboard included, has the same dark strip at the top (`assets/site-nav.js`): the home page, this week's plan, the IXL plan, the IXL Coach and the list of study pages. It stays on screen while the page scrolls, with the page's own bar just under it, so there is always a way back; on phones it uses short labels. On iPad and iPhone Safari the page runs under the clock and the folded address bar (`viewport-fit=cover`), so the strip stops just below them, and the screen above it takes the strip's colour instead of showing the page. Pages that scroll to an element themselves read its height from `--site-nav-h`. On pages that don't load the Arabic font (Tajawal) for their own text, the strip loads it.
- Easier on a phone, an iPad and a computer:
  - **Back to the top:** on a long page (over three screens), a round button appears at the bottom left once you are a screen and a half down (`assets/site-nav.js`). It is not printed, and the arcade, which has its own controls at the bottom, opts out with `data-top="0"`.
  - **Quick jumps:** under «ايش المطلوب؟» on the dashboard, five chips jump to the homework, the tests, the notices, the study pages and the past weeks.
  - **Short link names:** a link in a notice or a homework shows a short name («IXL: understanding integers», «vocabtest: definitions», «Google Drive») instead of the whole address, which stays in its title. Each name moves to the next line as one piece, and punctuation after a link stays outside it.
  - **Taller tap areas:** the links in the today box and in the weekly plans' evening jobs, the timetable's homework pills, the arcade's «افتح الصفحة» links, the dashboard's print button and the English word buttons are at least about 40 px tall. A long worked example on the weekly plans wraps between its steps on a narrow phone.
- The school calendar 2026/2027 is in `SCHOOL_DATES` (index.html):
  - **What it holds:** the start of the year, the MAP tests, the breaks (their dates, the school days off and the first day back), the parents' conference windows, the final exams of both terms and the last day.
  - **On the dashboard:** under the date, two chips count down to the next break and the next exams. «مواعيد وتنبيهات» shows what is on now or coming (the next three entries, and anything within 75 days). A folding table lists the whole year by term.
  - **Week numbers:** whole weeks off (`BREAK_WEEKS`, kept in step with `assets/site-nav.js`) are not counted, so the week after the Autumn Break is week 13. This is why the Qur'an quiz of week 13 falls after the break. During a break, the date line says which break it is and when school is back.
  - **The next school day:** skips breaks as well as weekends, so before a break the «المطلوب اليوم» box already shows a quiz on the first day back.
  - **Term 2:** if its weekly plans start again from week 1, change `TERM_START` for Term 2 in both files.
- Under the greeting, «المطلوب اليوم» says what to do now: tests on the next school day and graded projects one by one, homework that has been set grouped by due date (the next two dates), the nearest test with a countdown, and a link to tonight's jobs on the week's plan. A test on the day itself counts until 15:00 Jeddah time.
- Under it, «إنجازاتي» shows his level, today's goal, the streak of study days and the latest stickers, with a link to the album (see [Rewards](#rewards-points-stickers-and-celebrations)).
- Then «تقدّمه في خطة IXL» shows how many of the IXL plan's 210 skills are done, from the same ticks as the plan page (so the numbers match it): a bar with a line where the plan expects him by Saturday (every skill of the plan weeks so far), this week's skills, and a small bar for each subject. `IXL_SUBJECTS` in `index.html` lists the 210 skills by subject and must stay in step with `SKILLS` in `ixl/index.html`. It shows no IXL scores and no name.
- The study pages are grouped: "start here" (this week's plan, next week's once it is ready, the IXL plan and the practice sheet), then math, science, English and ICT, then older weekly plans. The IXL plan (below) adds one homework item a week from the week's first day, with how many of its skills are done; the plan page ticks the item when every skill of that week is done. Teacher assignments found on IXL can be added to `EXTRA` with a direct link to the skill.
- Notes on what to study or bring, items from the Weekly Study Plan or the class's EduPage messages that the public list doesn't have (`EXTRA`), due dates that the list leaves out but the Weekly Study Plan or Weekly Events give (`DUE_FIX`, for example the Be Well presentation on 1 Nov, which EduPage dates without a deadline), school calendar dates (`SCHOOL_DATES`) and class notices (`NOTICES`) are kept by hand near the top of the script in `index.html`. Nothing personal (messages to or about the student, reports, behaviour notes) goes on this public site.

## Rewards (points, stickers and celebrations)

`assets/rewards.js` makes studying on the site feel rewarding for an 11-year-old, without changing the pages themselves:

- **Points** come from the progress the pages already save and sync, so every device shows the same total: 50 for each tick on the dashboard and the week plans, 30 for each IXL skill that reached its target, 10 for each question in the practice sheet's best scores, 40 for each IXL Coach ready check passed, 20 for each lesson reviewed on the Quiz 2 page, and each XP point in the interactive lessons (a right answer is 10). Points, levels and stickers follow the highest total so far, so they never go down: unticking and ticking again doesn't count twice, and clearing the ticks takes nothing away.
- **Points once per question:** every page that gives points for answers (the five Quiz 2 reviews, Unit 4 Missions, the grammar and reading missions, the Word Quests and the Units 1–3 review, the four math labs, the science and ICT lessons and the English Tutor) gives a question's points only the first time it is answered right. The page remembers short hashes of the questions already rewarded (`xq`, synced with its progress) and says so when a repeat earns nothing. End-of-round points come only with a new best: a test or final's +20, new land or warm-up stars, a speed round beyond the best score, the reaction game's best time, the tutor's test above its best, and a skill understood the first time. The English letter's +30 comes once a day. So answering the same questions again in new rounds can't farm points; parts mastered and arcade time are unchanged.
- **Levels:** level *n* starts at 50·*n*·(*n*−1) points, so each takes 100 more than the one before. The titles praise effort, from مبتدئ to أسطورة.
- **Daily goal and streak:** the goal is 100 points a day, and 30 points make a day of the streak. Friday and Saturday never break the streak. Every 7 days in a row earn a shield (2 at most) that saves the streak on one missed school day.
- **Sticker album,** [`rewards/index.html`](rewards/index.html): four series of ten (space, desert and sea, sports, science and technology), each with common, rare and legendary stickers. The first three are a gift. After that a sticker opens every 200–380 points earned since the album started, and the order isn't shown, so the next one is a surprise. Once the album is full, every 500 points add a gold star. The page also shows the level, today's goal, the streak and the last two weeks' points, and tapping a sticker shows it again.
- **Celebrations** only happen for progress saved on this device within seconds of a tap. Pages also save as they open, and another device's progress only updates the numbers. A celebration can be:
  - a short toast with the points and a line that praises the work, never his cleverness, sometimes with a fun fact;
  - a sound from a tiny synth, with a few versions of each sound. It can be switched off on the card or the album, and stays silent when a lesson's own sound is off;
  - confetti, except with reduced motion;
  - a card for the daily goal, a new level, a new sticker, a finished series or a gold star.

  Right answers in the lessons get no toast of their own, because the lessons already celebrate them, but they count towards the goal.
- **Where it shows:** the dashboard's «إنجازاتي» card, and a chip at the end of the strip (from 820 px wide) that opens the album. The first visit to the dashboard on each device explains the system once.
- **Storage:**
  - `monmon-activity-v1` (synced) keeps each day's points per device in `days[date][device]`, so two devices never overwrite each other. It also keeps the highest total (`peak`) and the total when the album started (`start`). The start is only set once a page has the family's progress, so a device that is behind doesn't move it.
  - `monmon-fx-v1` (this device only) keeps the sound setting, the device's id and what this device already celebrated.
  - `assets/sync.js` sends a `monmon-saved` event for each save made on this device. `MonmonSync.peek()` reads a value without counting as the page's own read.
- **Why it works this way:**
  - Unexpected rewards and praise that tells the child something about the work keep or raise interest, while rewards promised in advance for doing a task can lower it (Deci, Koestner and Ryan, 1999). So the sticker order is a surprise, and nothing is promised for a particular task.
  - Praising effort rather than intelligence helps children keep going after mistakes (Mueller and Dweck, 1998).
  - Points, badges and levels have a moderate positive effect on learning (Sailer and Homner, 2020).

## Arcade (a reward for mastering parts)

[`arcade/index.html`](arcade/index.html) is a games room opened by study. Its ledger is `assets/arcade.js`, loaded on the pages that have parts: the Islamic, English, Arabic, science and social studies Quiz 2 reviews, the English Tutor, the grammar and reading missions, the Unit 4 Word Quest and Unit 4 Missions, the four math labs and the IXL Coach.

- **Earning:** each part mastered gives 5 minutes of play, once. A part is a review mode at 80% or more of a full round (or 12/15 in the Islamic mock, 16/20 in the Arabic mock, 24/30 in the English and science tests), a lab zone or a Word Quest land at 3 stars, a lab or Word Quest final at 80% or more, a Unit 4, grammar or reading mission passed (only once that track's Missions 1, 2 and 3 are all passed), a part of the English Tutor understood or its practice test at 80%, or an IXL Coach ready check. The first game is open from the start with 5 minutes, and each part mastered opens the next of the five games (Sky Drop, Build Rush, Storm Run, Target Rush and Fruit Rain, written for the site).
- **The first four games**, each with its own art drawn once into small canvases (`pic()`), soft glows and a pop-in banner for big moments:
  - **Sky Drop:** a sunny sky with parallax clouds and birds. Five stars in a row double them and ten treble them; a storm cloud ends the streak. Falling through a rainbow ring gives 25. A marker shows where the target is before it comes into view: the island with its palm tree, then a raft that moves.
  - **Build Rush:** each block is a floor with windows, hung from a crane. The sky changes with the tower's height: a sunny city, sunset, night with lit windows and the moon, then space with a ringed planet. There is a banner and confetti every 10 floors.
  - **Storm Run:** a meadow with flowers scrolls by, with bushes along the sides. The storm is a cloud wall with rain and lightning, and the edges of the screen glow red as it gets close. The boulders have shadows, the gems spin and the energy orbs glow.
  - **Target Rush:** a neon night with a striped sun and a moving grid. A ring around each target shows its time. Targets drift after 10 seconds. Hits shatter into neon pieces, and a meter shows the next combo multiplier. The last 5 seconds tick.
- **Fruit Rain** (the fifth, rarity «خارقة»): fruit falls from the sky and a swipe slices it; the halves show the fruit inside, with juice and splashes. A bomb sliced costs one of three hearts; a golden date is worth 50; an ice crystal slows time for 4 seconds; three or more fruit in one swipe is a combo. A round is 60 seconds and speeds up, with a fast wave every 15 seconds. The art (fruit, halves, the desert sky at dusk) is drawn by code into small canvases once, then reused.
- **No tricks:** a part counts when a page saves it as newly mastered on that device (the `monmon-saved` event: before → after), so parts mastered before the arcade existed, or mastered again, give nothing, and each part id is earned once. Play time is a balance spent second by second only while a game runs in front, and saved every second: going back, reloading or leaving never gives it back, a hidden page pauses, one window plays at a time (`monmon-arcade-live`, on the device only), and a changed clock can only move the day limit, not create time. At most 20 minutes a day.
- **Parent's free play:** a private link kept by the parent (`arcade/#free=<key>`) opens every game with no clock and no daily limit. It spends nothing and saves nothing, so the boy's balance, play time, best scores and the one-window lock are untouched; the page shows his current balance for reference. Only the SHA-256 of the key is in the page; without the key (or with a wrong one) the arcade is unchanged.
- **Saved** in `monmon-arcade-v1` (synced): the parts earned with their time, the play time per day per device (each device adds only its own), and the best score per game. The lobby has a parents' log of the parts and the minutes per day.

## Game feel

`assets/fx.js` is loaded on the study pages: the Islamic, English, Arabic, science and social studies reviews, the math, science, vocabulary and ICT pages.

- **Right answer:** the answer pops with sparks, and the points fly to the page's ⭐ chip.
- **Wrong answer:** the answer shakes and the screen flashes red.
- **Combos:** 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20 right answers in a row show a tilted combo banner in rarity colours.
- **How it hooks in:** it watches the feedback box every page already shows (`.fb.ok` / `.fb.no`) and the ⭐ chip going up, so no page has to call it.
- **Sounds and slide-in:** `data-sound="1"` adds short sounds and `data-enter=".run"` slides questions in. Only the five Quiz 2 reviews use them; the labs keep their own sounds and transitions.
- **Reduced motion:** with "reduce motion" on, nothing moves.

## Reading voice

`assets/voice.js` reads aloud on the six pages that speak: the Word Quests (Units 2 and 4, the Units 1–3 review), the English Tutor, the English Quiz 2 review, and the Arabic dictation trainer. It uses the device's own voices (the Web Speech API).

- **The clearest voice:** downloaded Premium or Enhanced voices on iPad, iPhone and Mac, Natural voices in Edge, Google voices in Chrome; never the novelty voices (Bad News, Bubbles, Grandpa…).
- **Voice panel:** the person button in the top bar (or «صوت القراءة» in the dictation box) lists the voices best first, sets the speed (أبطأ / عادي / أسرع) and remembers both on this device (`monmon-voice-v1`). When the device has only the old voices, it gives the steps to download a clearer one (on iPad: الإعدادات ← تسهيلات الاستخدام ← المحتوى المنطوق ← الأصوات).
- **Sentence by sentence:** long text is read one sentence at a time. It sounds more natural, and Chrome stops one long utterance after about 15 seconds.
- **Dictation like a teacher:** «أملِ عليّ» reads the whole sentence, then each short phrase twice with time to write, then the whole sentence again. A phrase never ends on a joining word (في، من، عن…), and «إيقاف» stops it at once.

## Progress sync between devices

Progress is saved in each browser's localStorage. Every page (the dashboard included) also loads `assets/sync.js` before its own script, which keeps that progress in step across the family's devices through a Firebase Realtime Database. Every device that opens the site syncs, with no link or sign-in: the family chose that over opening a link on each device, so the progress is open to anyone who opens the site. The page [`sync/index.html`](sync/index.html) shows whether this device syncs and when it last did, and can switch sync off (or back on) on this device. An older family link (`sync/#code=…`) still works and just switches sync on.

- **What syncs:** the keys in `KEYS` in `assets/sync.js`. These are the homework ticks (`monmon-done-v1`), the IXL plan ticks, the practice sheet's best scores, the saved progress of each study page, the IXL Coach's ready checks (`monmon-ixl-coach-v1`), the lessons reviewed for Quiz 2 (`monmon-quiz2-v1`), the arcade's parts and play time (`monmon-arcade-v1`), and the rewards' log of points (`monmon-activity-v1`). When a new page saves progress, add its key there. Its `xp` then counts towards the rewards too.
- **Sending:** ticks and best scores are sent within seconds. Quiz progress is sent a minute after the last answer, and at most 5 minutes after the first. Anything left is sent when the page is hidden or closed.
- **Receiving:**
  - While a page is in front, the database streams the time of each change to it.
  - Pages that are not open fetch the changes when they open or come back to the front.
  - The dashboard, the IXL plan, the practice sheet, the arcade and the Islamic Studies, English, Arabic, science and social studies reviews redraw in place. Other pages reload if nobody has touched them yet, and otherwise show a banner.
- **No undoing other devices' changes:** each save is turned into just the changes the page made since it last read the value. So a page still showing older progress never undoes another device's change. A device that already has progress when it first syncs adds what only it has; where both have a value, the family's wins.
- **Storage:** the family's node is `/f/<CODE>`, with `CODE` from `assets/sync.js`. Inside it:
  - `d` holds one entry per value, keyed by its path, for example `monmon-done-v1|done|<item id>`. Each segment is %-escaped for Firebase, and each value is stored as JSON text, so arrays and empty objects come back exactly as saved.
  - `t` is the server time of the last change.
- **Database rules:** they deny everything except reading and writing `f/<CODE>`:

  ```json
  {"rules": {".read": false, ".write": false, "f": {"<CODE>": {".read": true, ".write": true}}}}
  ```

  `CODE` is in `assets/sync.js`, a public file, so it is not a secret: anyone who opens the site can read and change the progress. To make it private again, change the path in the rules and in `CODE`, stop syncing automatically, and give each device a link with the new code instead.
